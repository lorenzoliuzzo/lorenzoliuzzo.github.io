Between measurements a quantum system does not jump. It evolves smoothly and reversibly, by a rule so simple that its solution is a recipe: find the energy eigenstates, and attach a phase to each.

# Unitary evolution

The third postulate says that the state of an isolated system at time $t$ is obtained from the state at time $0$ by a unitary operator, $|\psi(t)\rangle=U(t)|\psi(0)\rangle$, with $U(t)^\dagger U(t)=I$ so that norms, and therefore total probability, are preserved. If $U$ depends smoothly on $t$, it is generated by a Hermitian operator.

> The **Hamiltonian** $H$ is the Hermitian operator that generates time evolution. It represents the energy of the system, and its eigenvalues are the energy levels.
{: .definition #def-hamiltonian-operator title="Hamiltonian"}

> **Schrödinger equation.** The state of an isolated system obeys $i\hbar\,\dfrac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle=H|\psi(t)\rangle$. For a time-independent $H$ the solution is $|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-iHt/\hbar}|\psi(0)\rangle$.
{: .law #def-schrodinger-equation title="Schrödinger equation"}

The exponential of an operator is defined by its spectral decomposition, $e^{-iHt/\hbar}=\sum_ne^{-iE_nt/\hbar}|E_n\rangle\langle E_n|$, which is unitary because each $e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$ has modulus one. Reading it gives the standard recipe: expand the initial state in energy eigenstates, $|\psi(0)\rangle=\sum_nc_n|E_n\rangle$, and multiply each coefficient by its own phase.

> A **stationary state** is an energy eigenstate $H|E_n\rangle=E_n|E_n\rangle$. Under evolution it only acquires the global phase $e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$, so all probabilities, and the expectation value of every time-independent observable, are constant.
{: .definition #def-stationary-state title="Stationary state"}

A superposition of different energies is not stationary: the relative phase $e^{-i(E_m-E_n)t/\hbar}$ changes in time, and expectation values oscillate at the Bohr frequencies $(E_m-E_n)/\hbar$. This is the origin of every atomic spectral line.

# A two-level system

Take two states $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$ with $H=\hbar\begin{pmatrix}0&g\\g&\Delta\end{pmatrix}$: a coupling $g$ and an energy offset $\hbar\Delta$ (detuning) between the levels. Starting in $|0\rangle$, the probability to find $|1\rangle$ is the Rabi formula
$$ P_1(t)=\frac{4g^2}{\Delta^2+4g^2}\sin^2\!\Big(\frac{\Omega t}2\Big),\qquad\Omega=\sqrt{\Delta^2+4g^2}. $$
On resonance, $\Delta=0$, the population is transferred completely at time $\pi/2g$. With $\Delta=2g$ the peak is $\frac12$ and the oscillation is faster by $\sqrt2$.

{% include fig.html src="quantum-theory/rabi-oscillation" id="fig-rabi-oscillation" alt="Two oscillating curves of probability against time, one reaching 1 on resonance and a faster one reaching only one half when detuned." caption="Rabi oscillations of a two-level system. On resonance the population oscillates between $0$ and $1$ as $\sin^2gt$; a detuning of $\Delta=2g$ halves the amplitude and raises the frequency by $\sqrt2$." %}

The same formula describes a spin in a rotating field and an atom driven by a laser, and it is why a resonant pulse of the right length is a quantum gate: choosing $gt=\pi/2$ is a $\pi$ rotation of the Bloch sphere.

# The Heisenberg picture

The expectation value of an observable $A$ evolves as $\langle A\rangle_t=\langle\psi(0)|U^\dagger AU|\psi(0)\rangle$. One can instead move the evolution onto the operator, $A_H(t)=U^\dagger AU$, and leave the state fixed. Then $\dfrac{dA_H}{dt}=\dfrac{i}{\hbar}[H,A_H]$, the quantum version of Hamilton's equation with the commutator in the place of the Poisson bracket.

> A conserved quantity is an observable that commutes with the Hamiltonian: $[H,A]=0$ makes $A_H(t)$ constant. Together with Noether's theorem this connects symmetries to conservation laws in quantum theory.
{: .implication title="Conservation laws"}

> The Schrödinger equation does not give the time of a measurement outcome or explain why one outcome happens. It governs the evolution between measurements, and the measurement postulate takes over at each one.
{: .remark}

> To solve a time-dependent problem with a constant $H$, diagonalise $H$, expand the initial state in its eigenvectors and attach the phase $e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$ to each. When the system is driven, look for a rotating frame where $H$ is constant.
{: .rule}

# Recap

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Why is time evolution unitary?</summary>

Evolution must preserve probabilities, so the norm of the state is constant, and norm-preserving linear maps are unitary. The Hermitian generator of the family is the Hamiltonian.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Why is an energy eigenstate stationary?</summary>

Its only change is a global phase $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$, which affects no probability.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What does detuning do to Rabi oscillations?</summary>

It lowers the maximum transfer to $4g^2/(\Delta^2+4g^2)$ and raises the oscillation frequency to $\sqrt{\Delta^2+4g^2}$.
</details>
